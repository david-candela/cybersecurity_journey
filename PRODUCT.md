# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Primary: hiring managers and technical recruiters in cybersecurity (SOC analyst roles, security operations teams). They scan fast, looking for proof of genuine commitment and operational readiness. Secondary: cybersecurity peers and learners who may follow along or connect.

## Product Purpose

A personal learning journal and portfolio documenting David's career transition from 13 years at Apple into cybersecurity. The site makes one argument: this candidate is credible, thorough, and actively building real skills — not just collecting certifications. Success means a hiring manager leaves the site confident enough to move forward.

## Brand Personality

Precise, understated, ambitious. The voice is that of someone who came from a culture of craft (Apple) and brought that same exacting standard to a new field. Not loud, not performative — the work speaks.

## Anti-references

- The generic "dark hacker" aesthetic: Matrix green on black, terminal-green glows, neon-on-dark. The cybersecurity cliché that signals noise, not signal.
- Any visual treatment that collapses this into a resume template or corporate SaaS portfolio.

## Design Principles

1. **Let the work carry the weight.** No decorative noise. Every visual element serves a purpose. The content's depth should be more impressive than the design around it.
2. **Confident understatement.** Apple-level restraint: nothing gratuitous, nothing missing. Quality felt rather than announced.
3. **Show trajectory, not just status.** This is a transition in motion. The design should feel alive and moving, not frozen like a static resume.
4. **Warm expertise.** There is a human story here. The warmth of the palette and voice should prevent this from reading as cold technical documentation.
5. **Refuse the category reflex.** Cybersecurity portfolios all look the same. Distance from cliché is a competitive advantage.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Readable and usable. No formal WCAG target required, but good contrast and legible type are non-negotiable.
